Classes

Coursework at Texas A&M and before, most recent first. Descriptions follow the official TAMU course catalog; the impact notes cover what each class built toward, plus a few personal notes from along the way.

Fall 2026

ECEN 403

Electrical Design Laboratory I

The design process and project engineering as practiced in industry, taken on as a team: developing and documenting a project proposal, then building an execution and validation plan. The project starts here and continues through ECEN 404.

Impact:The start of senior design. The project my team proposes and plans this semester is the one we build, test and report on in ECEN 404.

ECEN 475

Introduction to VLSI Systems Design

Design and fabrication of microelectronic circuits, with an emphasis on very large scale integration (VLSI) digital systems. Uses current design methodologies and tools to design small- to medium-scale integrated circuits for fabrication.

Impact:Builds directly on ECEN 454, its prerequisite. Where 454 analyzed individual digital circuits, this course designs a complete integrated circuit meant to be fabricated.

CSCE 410

Operating Systems

How hardware and software evolved into today's operating systems, and how they are designed and built: algorithms for CPU scheduling, memory and general resource allocation, and process coordination and management, with case studies of several real operating systems.

Impact:Goes underneath the OS interface I programmed against in CSCE 313 (one of its prerequisites) to how the scheduler, memory manager and process management are actually built.

CSCE 481 · 1 credit

Seminar

Students investigate and report on topics of current interest in computer science.

Impact:Practice researching a current topic in the field and presenting it to others, instead of only learning settled material from a textbook.

ANTH 210

Social and Cultural Anthropology

A core anthropology course examining human cultures and societies — kinship, belief systems, economics, and social organization — through a cross-cultural, comparative lens.

Impact:A required look outside engineering — a reminder that the systems I build are used by people shaped by very different cultures and assumptions.

CSCE 411

Design and Analysis of Algorithms

Algorithms for numeric and non-numeric problems: design paradigms, analysis of time and space requirements, proving correctness, and NP-completeness and undecidability.

Impact:Combines its two prerequisites, CSCE 221's data structures and CSCE 222's proof techniques, to design algorithms and prove they are correct and efficient. It also covers how to recognize problems that likely have no efficient solution.

Spring 2026

ECEN 454

Digital Integrated Circuit Design

Analysis and design of digital devices and integrated circuits using MOS and bipolar technologies, with computer-aided simulation.

Impact:Takes the logic gates from ECEN 248 down to the transistors that implement them, and it's the prerequisite for designing a full chip in ECEN 475.

ECEN 449

Microprocessor Systems Design

Hardware and software design for 16/32-bit single-board computers: chip-select equations for memory boards, serial and parallel I/O interfacing, ROM and static and dynamic RAM, and assembly-language programming with stack models, subroutines and I/O processing.

Impact:Connects hardware and software at the lowest level: wiring memory and I/O to a processor, then writing the assembly that drives them.

ECEN 325

Electronics

Electronic systems and linear circuits; operational amplifiers and their applications; diodes, field-effect transistors and bipolar transistors; amplifiers and nonlinear circuits. Includes a weekly lab.

Impact:Moves from ECEN 214's ideal components to real semiconductor devices, and it's one of the required courses for starting senior design in ECEN 403.

CSCE 331

Foundations of Software Engineering

The fundamentals of larger-scale software development through team projects: techniques that make code easier to integrate and read, a variety of development tools, and hands-on software-engineering process, specification and documentation.

Impact:My team designed and built a full website together end-to-end, and this is where I learned how much one person's code affects everyone else's on a shared codebase. It's also where Git and GitHub went from a checkbox skill to something I now use daily for version control and real collaboration.

Fall 2025

13 credit hours
STAT 211

Principles of Statistics I

Data summaries, probability distributions, sampling distributions, hypothesis testing, and regression.

Impact:Statistics literacy that's paid off in every lab report and dataset since — knowing when a result is actually significant.

MATH 311

Topics in Applied Mathematics I

Linear algebra — matrices, vector spaces, eigenvalues — paired with vector calculus: gradient, divergence, curl, and the integral theorems.

Impact:The linear algebra here is the backbone behind everything from computer graphics to machine learning.

ECEN 314

Signals and Systems

Continuous-time and discrete-time signals and systems: time-domain characterization of linear time-invariant systems, Fourier analysis, filtering, sampling, and modulation techniques for communication systems.

Impact:The math behind every filter, sensor and communication link in ECE. It's also a prerequisite for ECEN 325 and for senior design.

CSCE 313 (Honors)

Introduction to Computer Systems

System support for application programs, both on a single machine and over a network: the OS–application interface, inter-process communication, system and network programming, and basic computer security, with hands-on lab assignments.

Impact:First class where the OS stopped being invisible — writing code that talks directly to processes and the network. It's the prerequisite for Operating Systems (CSCE 410).

ECEN 469

Advanced Computer Architecture

Advanced computer architectures: memory design, pipelining techniques, and parallel structures such as out-of-order cores and multiprocessors.

Impact:Learning advanced architecture alongside engineers from another country gave a broader, less US-centric view of how the industry builds hardware.

ECEN 350

Computer Architecture and Design

Using register transfer languages and simulation tools to describe and simulate how a computer operates; central processing unit organization, and input/output and memory system architectures.

Impact:Demystified what a CPU actually does between fetching an instruction and executing it — the biggest lightbulb moment of the degree so far.

Spring 2025

14 credit hours
MATH 308

Differential Equations

Ordinary differential equations, series solutions, Laplace transforms, and systems of differential equations.

Impact:The differential equations behind circuits and signals. It's a prerequisite for both ECEN 214 and ECEN 314.

ECEN 214

Electrical Circuit Theory

Resistive circuits, circuit laws, network reduction, and nodal and mesh analysis; an introduction to op-amps; circuits with inductors and capacitors; sinusoidal steady-state analysis and AC power; magnetically coupled circuits and the ideal transformer; resonance; and circuit simulation. Includes a weekly lab.

Impact:The circuit analysis every later hardware course assumes. It's a prerequisite for ECEN 314 and ECEN 454.

CSCE 222

Discrete Structures for Computing

Mathematical foundations from discrete mathematics for analyzing computer algorithms, both for correctness and performance, plus an introduction to models of computation, including finite state machines and Turing machines.

Impact:Gave me the proof-and-logic toolkit for reasoning about whether an algorithm is actually correct, which CSCE 411 builds on directly.

CSCE 221

Data Structures and Algorithms

Specifying and implementing basic abstract data types and their algorithms: stacks, queues, lists, sorting and selection, searching, graphs, and hashing. Covers the performance tradeoffs between implementations and asymptotic analysis of running time and memory use.

Impact:The data structures learned here show up in essentially every technical interview and every serious codebase since. It's the prerequisite for CSCE 313 and CSCE 411.

Freshman year at Texas A&M

ECEN 248

Introduction to Digital Systems Design

Combinational and sequential digital system design techniques, and the design of practical digital systems. Includes a weekly lab.

Taught basic gates and Boolean logic, expanding into the more physical side of technology — exciting as a Computer Engineering major.

Impact:The first time hardware stopped being a black box. It's the prerequisite for ECEN 350, ECEN 449 and ECEN 454.

CSCE 120

Program Design and Concepts

Building on prior programming to analyze, design and implement programs that solve problems: basic and aggregate data types, encapsulation and abstraction, dynamic memory without memory leaks, and error-handling strategies for more secure and robust programs.

Taught me the basics of C++, which felt similar to Java.

Impact:Built the C++ foundation that later systems and algorithms courses assume. It's a prerequisite for CSCE 221 and ECEN 350.

ENGR 102

Engineering Lab I: Computation

The required first-year engineering computation course at Texas A&M — Python for engineering problem solving, covering numerical methods, data analysis, and simulation.

My first college coding class, and my first time using Python. A project I remember fondly: parsing 50 years of weather data from a CSV file to find averages, maxes, and mins.

Impact:First real taste of using code to solve an engineering problem instead of just an assignment — turning raw data into an actual answer.

High school

Senior year

AP Computer Science A

A college-level AP course in object-oriented programming with Java, covering classes, arrays, and algorithms, and ending in the AP exam.

My favorite class of high school. Coincided with Robotics; for the final project, my partner and I built a Snake game from scratch in Java.

Impact:Proved I could hold my own in a full object-oriented language and helped me decide to pursue Computer Engineering in college.

Junior year

Computer Science B

A non-AP course typically used as a stepping stone toward AP Computer Science A, focused on JavaScript fundamentals.

Taken fully online because of COVID-19. Taught the basics of JavaScript, which prepared me for AP Computer Science A.

Impact:Built the JavaScript fluency that made the jump into AP Computer Science A much smoother.

Sophomore year

AP Computer Science Principles

An introductory, language-agnostic AP course covering the big ideas of computing — algorithms, data, the internet, and computing's impact — built around creating projects rather than mastering one language.

This was my first ever coding class. It used Scratch, a block-coding website, to make projects.

Impact:Opened the door to computer science as a career path and showed that programming could be creative, not just technical.
